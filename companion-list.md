* Thompson, *Mind in Life* (reread) — The systematic enactivist account: consciousness as a living, temporal, world-involving process. Read years ago before the project gave it a reason; now it's the deep background. Ask what the theory names that the novels only enact.
* Thompson, *Waking, Dreaming, Being* (new) — The self across sleep, dream, and death; the temporal structure of experience. The most on-target chapters either author wrote. Digests Husserl's retention-protention so I don't fight the original. Ask how this maps to principle 3 — the texture of remembering, anticipating, attending.
* Ricoeur, *Time and Narrative*, Vol. 1 (new) — Time-consciousness meets narrative form: the single most on-point work for structuring temporal experience without plot. Argues like a systematic thinker, not a verbal phenomenologist. Ask what it says about emplotment that the mode is trying to refuse.
* Noë, *Action in Perception* (reread) — The tightest analytic-friendly statement of perception as enactive. The most rigorous and most congenial to my training. Ask what "the reader's mind becoming the work's consciousness" owes to perception being world-involving rather than skull-bound.
